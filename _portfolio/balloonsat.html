Project Objective

This project consisted of designing and implementing a multi-functional light source tracking system that could be capable of monitoring environmental and system level parameters. The main objective of this project involved constructing a model of a real-life satellite payload capable of tracking actual sunlight while being able to resist hazards and obtain crucial, live data in space. Therefore, in order to accomplish this goal, five specific subsystems were built in order to satisfy the requirements for this project. These subsystems are as follows:

  1. Power Source Subsystem
  2. Power Monitoring Subsystem
  3. Sun Tracking Subsystem
  4. Temperature Subsystem
  5. Telemetry Subsystem

For the sake of summarizing, information regarding detailed explanations, procedures, component schematics, and such for this Microcomputer Instrumentation project, please refer to the technical report prepared by the team foundhere.

1. Power Source Subsystem

This subsystem consisted on developing a power source capable of providing the necessary voltage and current to the entire structure. Due to important constraints such as voltage limits and analog circuitry, DC power was converted through positive and negative voltage regulators. This provided a balanced power environment for the other subsystems that needed positive and negative power rails, mostly seen for many of the operational amplifiers used. Therefore the primary objective of this subsystem lied in supplying 5V to both BalloonSat amplifiers, as well as supplying 7V to the stepper motor.

The components used were as follows:

  • Three 9V Batteries: Used for supplying DC power input into voltage regulator and tracking subsystem
  • LM317 Positive Voltage Regulator: Provides an adjustably stable positive power output
  • LM337 Negative Voltage Regulator: Provide an adjustably stable negative power output
  • 0.1 and 1 µF Tantalum Capacitors: Improve voltage stability and regulate voltage outputs along with potentiometer
  • Resistors: 330 Ω resistor for LM317 and 150 Ω resistor for LM337

2. Power Monitoring Subsystem

This subsystem had the main objective of tracking the voltage levels of the batteries used to power the system as a whole, along with the rate of power drawn from the batteries. It tracks the voltage levels through two inverting amplifiers which result in a total net gain of 1/3, while the power draw monitoring system was intended to measure current being pulled through a shunt resistor, though that latter part had its difficulties.

For the first amplifier:

  • Two 100KΩ resistors
  • One 200KΩ resistor
  • TL082 Operational Amplifier

For the second amplifier:

  • Two 39kΩ resistors
  • TL082 operational amplifier

Schematics of how power is sent through amplifiers, and how net gain turns to 1/3, check href="/files/"lab maual for TL082 amplifier and the official technical report.

3. Tracking Subsystem

For this subsystem, the primary goal was to determine the angular displacement of any given light source projected onto the from some given initial position. The main operation lied on three photodiodes being positioned on a 3D base, separated by 0.625 inches and 50 degrees apart, with the idea that they would function as light receptors. Whenever a light source would strike the 3D mount, depending on which LED would pick the strongest signal, the entire mount would rotate in that direction. A target prediction was also enabled in case that the photodiodes would stop picking light readings, the mount would continue to rotate in the last direction until a new reading would be picked up.

The components for this subsystem included:

  • Three white photodiodes
  • Three transimpedance amplifiers (schematics)
  • Three 330Ω resistors
  • Stepper motor (schematics)
  • H-Bridge (schematics)
  • TL082 operational amplifier
  • 10MΩ resistor

4. Temperature Subsystem

The main purpose of this subsystem, where I took the main lead, was to determine the temperature readings across the entire payload, which in this case would be the stepper motor rotating the 3D mount whenever light would be reflected onto it. This proved to be crucial since it is relevant to determine if there are temperature fluctuations within the payload which could affect the quality of the mission, which is what this project is intending to simulate. The important requirement that was needed to be satisfied was that this subsystem would be able to operate within -20°C to 60°C degrees. It was also important to have this data sent to the microcontroller, which would be converted from ADC counts into actual temperature and sent to telemetry system through UART (see technical report).

The components used for this subsystem were as follows:

  • ATmega644PA microcontroller: Received temperature readings in ADC counts
  • 2 LM05-103K NTC thermistors: Used for temperature sensing
  • UART: Transmitter for sending data to MATLAB ground station
  • TL082 operational amplifiers: Non-inverting amplifier setup

5. Telemetry Subsystem

This subsystem had the main objective consisted on transmitting all data collected from each subsystem in real time, simulating what a real ground station would pick up on a satellite payload being sent on a space mission. Though issues were present when collecting accurate data, this subsystem proved helpful by allowing the team to observe how, or if, the entire system was functioning accordingly, and whether the expected result were being obtained or not.
